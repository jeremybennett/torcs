Open Racing Simulator

What is Open Racing Simulator?

Hi. Welcome to the Open Racing Simulator robot tutorial. As you can tell from the screenshot, this is not about Celtic torcs — it is about writing computer-controlled race car drivers.
Open Racing Simulator is a community fork of TORCS (The Open Racing Car Simulator), a moddable open-source racing simulation that lets you drive against computer-controlled opponents. But that's not the whole story. When you want to go beyond driving yourself, you can develop your own computer-controlled driver (also called a robot) in C or C++. This tutorial will teach you how to get, compile, install and run the simulator within Linux, and how to start developing your own driver. Open Racing Simulator is open source (GNU General Public License Version 2), so you get all the sources for free. This tutorial is aimed toward robot development with Linux.

Features

Choose from 42 different cars, 30 tracks, more than 50 opponents to race against. Steer with a joystick or steering wheel, if it's supported by your platform. It is also possible to drive with the mouse or the keyboard, but it's not easy. Graphic features lighting, smoke, skidmarks and glowing brake disks. The simulation features a simple damage model, collisions, tire and wheel properties (springs, dampers, stiffness, ...), aerodynamics (ground effect, spoilers, ...) and much more. The gameplay allows different types of races from the simple practice session up to the championship. Enjoy racing against your friends in the split screen mode with up to four human players.

Platforms

Supported platforms are Linux (x86 and ppc are known to work), FreeBSD and Windows XP/2000/98.

Credits

The original TORCS simulator was developed by Eric Espié, Christophe Guionneau and other contributors. Open Racing Simulator is a community fork maintained by contributors on GitHub. Visit the upstream TORCS site for upstream history.
